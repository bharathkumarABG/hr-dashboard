How to use this dashboard
A live view of Adamsbridge's workforce — who is here, who is joining, who is leaving and how hiring is going — built from the Employee records and the Recruitment Team Master Tracker. Every number is calculated from those two sources; where something cannot be calculated honestly it shows "—".
  1. Start on Org Health Check. The top card lists what is flagged this week.
  2. Use the question finder below to jump straight to the tab and card that answers what you are asking.
  3. Slice with the filters at the top of each tab — the table below explains how they behave.

Insights (inside Org Health Check)The whole dashboard read out in plain lines, section by section. Last section of Org Health Check.
What is on it
  • Press Get insights for observations on Overall picture, Workforce, Attrition, Employee survey, Structure, Capacity and Hiring.
  • Each line is calculated from the live numbers by fixed rules (no AI, nothing leaves the dashboard) and links to the tab behind it.
  • Red and amber dots mark a number outside the rules; green is inside them; grey is for information.
How to slice and dice
  • Department and BU Head dropdowns; the read-out regenerates for the slice.
What the rules are
  • A month is flagged when exits are at least 1.5 times the previous 11-month average (and at least 3 higher).
  • A department is flagged when its 12-month exits are at least 1.3 times the overall share of headcount and at least 3 points higher (departments of 30 or more people).
  • Exit concentration is flagged when the top three departments' share of exits is 10 points above their share of headcount.
  • Early exits are flagged at 20% or more of leavers within 90 days.
  • Managers, capacity and hiring lines use the rules already listed in this guide.

Employee survey (ESAT 2026)
  • The last section on this tab follows the shared Department filter: headline survey numbers, lowest statements, most chosen improvements, and improvements against exit reasons.
  • With Overall selected, a table lists every survey group side by side.
How it's worked outThe score blends seven things equally: whether headcount is growing or shrinking, how much voluntary attrition there is, whether managers have too many people reporting to them, how performance ratings look this cycle versus last, how many people hold a real senior leadership title relative to the size of the workforce, and — among people who left this year — two separate reads on how soon they left: within their first 3 months (often a hiring or onboarding mismatch) and between 3 months and a year (often a manager, team, or compensation issue instead). If there isn't enough real data for a piece — most often performance ratings for a small department — it's left out rather than guessed, and the score is based on whichever of the others actually have data.

Reference — assumptions and thresholdsHow every red, amber and green is decided. Open when you want the rule behind a colour.
Every rule below drives a real color you'll see somewhere on this dashboard. Where a rule comes from an external, named source, that's stated — where it's a working default this dashboard set rather than an independently sourced figure, that's stated too, plainly, not dressed up as more authoritative than it is. Wherever you see a colored badge with a dotted underline, you can click it for the specific real numbers behind that exact reading, for that exact person or department — not just the general rule.
Manager span of control
Team LeadAgreed structure: 10 to 12 production team members. Green up to 12, amber 13 to 15, red 16 or more. Group-specific interim limits (for example Balaji Loganathan's group, 20) are listed on the Assumptions & Indicators page.
Assistant ManagerAgreed maximum of 25 direct reports, including 2 Team Leads. Green up to 25, red above 25. Production and SME-level people reporting directly are expected here, so they are not a structural gap.
Manager, Senior ManagerRated on the whole team beneath them (all levels): Manager about 50 (green up to 50, amber 51 to 60, red above 60), Senior Manager about 100 (green up to 100, amber 101 to 120, red above 120). The amber band is a working tolerance on "about".
Subject Matter Expert, Senior Analyst (Insurance Summarization)An SME is an individual-expert role, so any report is flagged. In Insurance Summarization, Senior Analysts with people reporting to them are rated on the Team Lead limits (green up to 12, amber 13 to 15, red above 15).
Director, VP and aboveNot rated on span size. Real direct-report and team size are still shown as plain numbers.
"Doing a lower role" flagA separate signal from span size. For a Manager, Senior Manager or above, having individual contributors reporting directly (with no Assistant Manager or Team Lead between) is flagged on its own. An Assistant Manager with production-level people directly is expected and not flagged.
Capacity mix (billable vs. management)Scoped to the real billable verticals only (Accounting, Insurance Summarization, MRC, Lease Abstraction, Legal Services). For the real billable individual-contributor headcount, how many of each level (Team Lead, Assistant Manager, Manager, and so on) are actually needed — computed independently at each rung straight from the base headcount, using the agreed structure (12 production members per Team Lead, 2 Team Leads per Assistant Manager, 2 Assistant Managers per Manager, 2 Managers per Senior Manager) — compared against how many we actually have. Each rung's "needed" number is not chained off the real, actual count at the rung below, so a real gap at one level doesn't quietly distort what "needed" looks like further up. A rung is only ever flagged as having more than needed once the real base headcount is at least 20 people — a small or newly-formed team is never flagged just because its raw numbers look distorted.
Attrition and exits
What counts as a real exitOnly people whose status has actually reached "Inactive." Someone "Serving Notice Period" is still working today and still counts as active headcount everywhere else on this dashboard — so their exit doesn't count until it's actually happened, even if a future last-working-date is already on file.
Voluntary attrition rateGreen under 8%, amber 8-15%, red above 15%. Counts voluntary exits only — people who left on their own, not people let go — since the two call for different action. Used consistently everywhere this rate is shown: Attrition tab, Org Health Check, Manager Health, Dept Health.
Immediate exits (under 3 months)Green under 25% of that period's exits, amber 25-50%, red above 50%. Usually points to a hiring or onboarding mismatch, not a management issue. This is the threshold used on Org Health Check, Manager Health, and the Attrition tab's junior-exit card — see the separate Diversity entry below for one place this number is currently different. The Attrition tab also carries a dedicated Monthly / YTD / Annualised table for this same under-90-day group, built with the same real mechanics as the main attrition table above it — the rate is against the whole month's average headcount, not just that month's new joiners.
Early-tenure exits (3 months to a year)Same green/amber/red bands as immediate exits above, but a genuinely different signal — often points to a manager, team, or compensation issue instead of a hiring mismatch.
AbscondingGreen at zero cases, amber at exactly one, red at two or more, per manager.
Total exits this month (Attrition tab KPI)Red above 15 real exits, amber at 15 or fewer. This one is binary — there's no green state for this specific KPI card.
"New Requirement" (Backfill vs. Exits table)Green at zero, amber if 1 or 2 real exits have no matching backfill request yet, red above 2.
Diversity
Gender-specific immediate / early-tenure exitsCurrently different from the main version above: green under 20%, amber 20-40%, red above 40%, rather than the 25%/50% used everywhere else these two signals appear. This inconsistency is real, not a documentation gap — it's flagged here honestly rather than hidden, and is worth a decision on whether the Diversity tab should be brought in line with the rest of the dashboard.
Talent Acquisition
Real Avg Time to FillA genuine measurement, not an estimate: real days from a specific requisition's real Req received date to a specific candidate's real DOJ, for every candidate in the Final Select Candidates Sheet whose Candidate Status is "Joined." Different from the source-to-DOJ figure shown elsewhere on this dashboard, which is a proxy based on when sourcing began, used only because Base Sheet itself has no reliable requisition-linked date at all.
When hiring is called outCalled out only when there is no traction: open positions in a department where no candidate has cleared L1 (with how many are at L1), requisitions with no candidate pipeline at all, offers accepted more than 60 days ago without joining (notice periods of 60 to 90 days are normal), and hiring pace below 20% over 6 months. Good hiring pace is not called out.
Active pipeline per requisitionRed at zero real candidates in active pipeline for that role, amber under 3, green at 3 or more.
Open positions vs. real demand (Org Health Check)Green if nothing is open (fully staffed against target), red if open positions exceed 60% of total real demand (Target), amber in between.
Other signals
Retention RiskGreen under 5% of a manager's current team on notice, amber 5-10%, red above 10%. Forward-looking — a different question from attrition, which is who's already gone.
Leadership DensityGreen under 5% of a population holding a real VP, AVP, Senior Manager, Director, or Head title, amber 5-10%, red above 10%.
Workforce growthGreen if net headcount change is positive, amber -5% to 0%, red below -5%.
Good performers lost (Org Health Check)Among that cycle's rated voluntary leavers, red if 50% or more were rated 4 or higher (the standard India-market bar for "exceeds expectations" on a 5-point scale), amber if 25% or more, green otherwise. A different question from the attrition rate — this asks whether the people leaving were the people worth keeping.
Reference — a few things worth knowingVoluntary vs involuntary exits, Monthly / YTD / Annualised, how filters carry across tabs.
Voluntary vs. involuntary exitsAttrition rates on this dashboard count voluntary exits only — the standard HR convention, since people who leave on their own and people who are let go call for different action. If an exit's type wasn't recorded, it's left out of the rate rather than guessed either way.
Monthly, YTD, and Annualised attritionThese are three different, real ways of reading the same underlying exits — not three separate opinions. The Attrition tab and the Insights reports both include a short table explaining exactly how each is worked out and when to use which one.

Senior managers — Level 5 and above — span, retention, absconding & early-tenure signals (shared filter)
Span is direct reports only, for every manager — indirect headcount (everyone under the managers who report to this person) is shown as its own separate column, since it's real and worth seeing, but it isn't what this rating is based on. A manager-of-managers gets a different threshold than a manager of individual contributors (amber above 6, red above 8 direct reports, vs. red above 10 for individual-contributor managers), since overseeing other managers is a different job — but both are rated on direct reports, never the size of the pyramid underneath. Retention Risk is forward-looking — % of this manager's current team currently Serving Notice Period, i.e. people who've already resigned but haven't left yet. Voluntary attrition is historical — people who already left, shown as a real percentage with the real count and the real year it's based on. That year is the most recent one with genuine activity for that specific manager's team, so it can differ row to row — not a typo if two managers show different years side by side.
